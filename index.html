03 · What we did · The finding

What seven actions taught us: it is a coverage gap

Capacity was added and the failures continued, so the cause is consumption inside the window, not disk. And every failure happened in hours that no one is on shift to see.

FINDING ONE
Consumption, not capacity
Disk was added to the transaction log and the failures continued. Something inside the window is consuming log space faster than any headroom can absorb. Long-running queries, parallel schedules and memory pressure remain the three suspects.
FINDING TWO
Timing is the pattern
Every failure sits between 5 and 10 PM CT, after the day shift and before the morning. The first failure landed the day after the last production push, so that push is under line-by-line review.
FINDING THREE
Rights and reach
Clearing a blocking session, restarting a job or reading wait and lock statistics needs DBA rights the pod does not hold. From the outside, root cause is slow and uncertain. From the inside, it is a specialist's routine.

One person on a day shift cannot see a 6 PM failure, and nobody should have to. That is the gap. The next scene closes it.

04 · What we propose · The server

A database SME alongside your DBA, and three steps beside it

Not a replacement for anyone at RRTS. Assistance in the hours no single person can cover, with the pod continuing on the engineering side.

The proposal · a database SME alongside your DBA
A dedicated SQL Server subject matter expert from the Chennai ODC, on shift through the 5 to 10 PM CT window and overnight, working with your in-house DBA rather than around him.
Evening coverOn shift when the failures happen. Blocking sessions killed and jobs restarted on the spot.
Morning hand-offYour DBA starts the day with a clean server and a written note of what happened overnight.
Root cause, from the insideLog, wait and memory diagnostics that only a DBA can run, shared as findings, not tickets.
Zero unplanned outagesThe target for the window, measured and reported at every coffee meet.
Assistance, not replacement. One person covering a 24-hour server is not a fair ask of anyone. Two shifts, one server, one team.
Three steps the pod takes beside it
1
Full analysis of the 5 to 10 PM CT window
Log and pointer analysis of every job in the window. Long-running queries, dependency chains and memory traces captured and shared with your DBA.
2
Line-by-line review of the latest production push
The first failure landed the day after the last deployment. Every query change in that push is being traced against the failing jobs.
3
Reschedule and alert
Remaining dev jobs moved out of the peak window and server-health alerting added, so a failure is seen the moment it happens rather than the next morning.
These reduce failures. The SME is what turns them into zero-outage operations and a root cause found from the inside.
